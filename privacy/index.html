Chillar privacy policy

Last updated 12 October 2026. This covers the Chillar browser extension, version 1.3 and later.

Chillar works on your computer. It has no account, no server and no tracking, and it sends nothing about what you browse to anyone.

What Chillar reads

To do its job, Chillar reads the pages you open, on your computer: checkboxes and fees on checkout pages, free-trial sign-ups, totals in dollars, euros or pounds, no-cost EMI plans you pick, and hotel booking confirmations. What it reads stays on your computer.

What Chillar keeps

In your browser's own storage, on your computer:

  • your settings, including your card's forex markup;
  • the total it has saved you, counts of what it caught, and a list of the 50 most recent catches;
  • free trials it is reminding you about;
  • hotels you asked it to watch, with the price you saw;
  • proof of each cancellation it made for you: the confirmation page's text and a screenshot;
  • short notes so it doesn't repeat itself: which sites it already showed a message on recently, sites that asked you to pay when you downloaded, and the hotel page you were on when you pressed Book (kept for a few hours, so it can offer to watch the price).

Removing Chillar from your browser deletes all of it.

What Chillar downloads

  • Today's exchange rates, from api.frankfurter.dev (or open.er-api.com if that is down).
  • A public list of cancel steps for popular services, from this website.

These requests carry nothing about you or your browsing. Like any web request, the server sees your IP address.

When Chillar opens pages for you

  • When you press "Cancel it for me", Chillar opens that service's website in a tab you can see, as you would. It never types passwords or one-time codes.
  • For hotels you asked it to watch, it opens the hotel page in a background tab once a day to read the price, then closes it.
  • "Post on X" in the share card opens X only when you press it.

On-device AI

On computers where Chrome offers its built-in AI model, the cancel helper can use it to find the next step on a page. That model runs on your computer; the page is not sent anywhere.
